# Threats to validity

To identify potential sources of bias and error in our design, we note the following limitations openly. **Scope and generalizability:** all findings rest on one open-source repository (Commitizen), so we cannot claim transferability to other domains, team sizes, or closed-source settings without replication. **Metric and temporal choices:** we built weekly time series and treated issue closures as the only vital sign; spikes in reviews, comments, or commits may carry equal or greater lifecycle meaning, so our definition of “anomalous” may miss or mislabel important periods. **Anomaly detection:** rolling IQR was chosen for its fit to the closure distribution and interpretable bands, but threshold settings (window length, multiplier) are researcher decisions, early weeks suffer from cold-start effects, and a statistical outlier is not the same as a substantively important event—other detectors might flag different weeks. **Text labelling:** severity and component labels come from hand-crafted regex lexicons over issue titles (and related commit text); this white-box approach aids transparency but embeds our own categorisation bias, is not validated against expert ratings, and may systematically misread Conventional Commit wording. **Utility evaluation:** the vitalizing sublog is defined by the same anomaly pipeline, so errors at detection propagate into process mining; F1 compares models to the log they were mined from, absolute precision remains low for several algorithms, the random control matches case count but not calendar context, and we report descriptive contrasts without formal hypothesis tests. We state these threats so readers can judge how far our results support lifecycle-informed sublog selection versus illustrating one coupled, interpretable pipeline on a single project.
